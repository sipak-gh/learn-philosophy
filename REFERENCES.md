# References

This file lists the sources behind the claims in this course: dates, attributions, translations, and the readings of each thinker the chapters rely on. Use it to check a claim or to read further. Quotations from public-domain books were checked word for word against the Project Gutenberg texts bundled in [readings/](readings/README.md); the Gutenberg page for each book is listed under the chapters that quote it. The exceptions are listed under their chapters: Anselm's *Proslogion* (Deane's translation, from the Fordham Internet Medieval Sourcebook), Bentham's *Introduction* and *Anarchical Fallacies* (Internet Archive scans), Aristotle's *Metaphysics*, *Physics* and *De Anima* and the Chinese philosophers Mencius and Xunzi (quoted through A. E. Taylor's and Herbert Giles's public-domain books, and labeled so in the chapters), and the *Tractatus* (from the Project Gutenberg PDF, which is not bundled). A few short quotations from modern books still in copyright are quoted as fair use and were checked against well-attested printings. Books still in copyright are listed under each chapter's **Books** heading as plain bibliographic entries.

Each online source has two links: **original** (the live page) and **archived** (a fixed copy, in case the original moves or changes). For most pages the archived copy is a snapshot in the Internet Archive's [Wayback Machine](https://web.archive.org/). The Wayback Machine does not return pages from the Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy, so for every SEP entry the archived link is instead the SEP's own dated archive edition, `https://plato.stanford.edu/archives/fall2026/entries/<slug>/`, which the encyclopedia keeps unchanged and recommends for citation. Four links have no snapshot anywhere and are marked "archived: none available": two SEP entries too new to be in any archive edition ("Friedrich Engels" and "Descartes and the Pineal Gland"), and two Internet Archive scans of Bentham, which are themselves archive pages. The Internet Encyclopedia of Philosophy has withdrawn its "Renaissance Humanism" article; the archived link preserves it.
